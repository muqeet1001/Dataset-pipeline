3. IMPLEMENT THE PER-RECORD FLOW

One invocation must:
  read the next dataset record
  -> generate or resume its accepted MHR body
  -> select a shirt and pants from the matching male/female catalog using a seed
  -> grade and place the outfit
  -> run tight, medium and loose simulations on the SAME body
  -> validate, export and record all three results
  -> persist a durable handoff to the next pipeline stage
  -> continue to the next record.

Keep style and fabric fixed across the three fits; change documented garment
rest dimensions and ease. Persist selection/seed so resume never reselects clothes.
There must be no per-record upload, interactive garment placement or manual click.
Use a long-lived GPU worker to avoid reloading an engine for every person.
Do not implement video generation, motion sequences, model training, deployment
services or other future stages. Diagnostic screenshots are part of validation.

4. NEWTON SOLVERS AND GARMENT CONSTRUCTION

Inspect upstream:
  newton/examples/cloth/example_cloth_style3d.py
  newton/_src/solvers/style3d/solver_style3d.py
  newton/tests/test_solver_style3d.py
  newton/tests/test_cloth.py
  newton/tests/test_collision_cloth.py
  newton/_src/solvers/vbd/solver_vbd.py

The Style3D example imports a garment, physical panel coordinates and a collider.
It is an example using fixed vendor assets, not an automatic MHR fitter. The
open-source Newton SolverStyle3D is the candidate here; do not confuse it with
requiring a separate commercial Style3D application.

Build simple short-sleeve shirt and long-pants templates, with suitable male and
female catalog pools. A single template per pool is acceptable for the first
pilot, but report its limited variety. Define seams, openings, rest geometry,
physical panel coordinates, mass/thickness and fabric presets. Appearance UVs
are not automatically fabric coordinates in metres. Model any waistband support
or elastic behavior explicitly. Avoid holding an entire garment against the skin.

Find neck, shoulders, armpits, waist, hips, crotch and limbs using validated MHR
landmarks/segmentation. Keep MHR vertex indices separate from SMPL/CAESAR indices.
Align openings with correct body parts and ensure shirt/pants layer ordering.
Prepare sensible low-penetration initial geometry; grossly entangled garments
must not be accepted just because a solver can move vertices.

Preserve the body as a static collider with its full dimensions unchanged. Convert
cm to m through a recorded transform and validate inverse/export transforms.
Both garments must participate in a common collision space. Exercise cloth-body,
self and shirt-pants contact, including triangle/edge crossings; do not simulate
the two garments independently and assume the combined outfit is valid.

Use actual gravity, stretch/shear/bending, damping, friction and thickness. Choose
bounded steps/substeps/iterations and a measured settling criterion. Check against
the full MHR mesh even if a simpler collider is used during coarse placement.
Do not hide the body, inflate collision distances or shrinkwrap cloth to conceal
fit failures. Record retries and failure reasons.

5. THREE FIT CONDITIONS

Grade shirt torso/sleeves and pants waist/hips/thighs/lengths while preserving the
same style and fabric. Keep intended tight/medium/loose labels distinct from the
measured resulting fit. The earlier primary-girth ease bands of 0-6%, 6-14% and
14-30% were prototype targets, not universal standards; calibrate sensible profiles
against actual results. Negative ease requires appropriate stretch validation.
Measure the body and rest garment at compatible anatomical locations. Changing
stiffness, clearance or body size alone does not create the required fit variants.
